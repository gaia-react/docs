GAIA does not run everything on one model. Your session's model handles the conversation, the decisions, and every step that needs you. When a workflow hands a step to a [sub-agent](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/sub-agents) (a separate Claude instance with its own fresh context), the workflow names the sub-agent's model, chosen for that kind of work. This page lists every one of those choices.

## How GAIA picks a model

Every choice follows one table, kept in your project at `wiki/concepts/Workflow Doctrine.md`. GAIA ranks models by how well they fit the work: how much reasoning it needs, and how closely the model has to stick to its instructions. Price decides only between two models that fit equally well.

| Kind of work | Model | Why it fits |
|---|---|---|
| Pulling out, sorting, or listing items by a written rule | Haiku | A rule decides each item, and the result can be counted or checked. |
| Following a template, a rule id, or a written convention: scaffolding, or a fix for a specific lint or accessibility rule | Haiku | The template already holds the answer. More reasoning adds nothing. |
| Reading widely and making a call on each item: research, checking a finding, verifying | Sonnet | The instructions carry the judgment, and Sonnet applies it one item at a time without straying from them. |
| A bounded change with a written plan and a check behind it | Sonnet | The plan carries the judgment, and Sonnet sticks to the plan. |
| Review: finding what a change got wrong | Opus | It needs sustained judgment and a wide view. A missed defect costs more than the price difference. |
| Specs, plans, and design or architecture decisions | Opus, or Fable\* | One weak choice here carries into everything built on it. |

<Aside type="note" title="GAIA never chooses Fable on its own">
A step runs on Fable only when your session is on Fable and the step uses your model, or when you pick Fable at `/gaia-plan`'s planner question.
</Aside>

Some steps never leave your session, whatever kind of work they are: a step that needs to ask you something (a sub-agent cannot ask you questions), small work, and quick edit-run-fix loops, where handing off costs more than it saves.

### Effort

GAIA never sets effort. Every sub-agent runs at your session's `/effort` level, the same as work your session does itself, so a low-effort session reviews at low and a high-effort one at high. `CLAUDE_CODE_EFFORT_LEVEL` overrides the level on a machine. GAIA chooses the model. You choose the effort.

## Skills that set a model

Six skills set Haiku in their frontmatter: the four scaffolders (`new-component`, `new-route`, `new-hook`, `new-service`) and the two fix skills (`eslint-fixes`, `a11y-fixes`). When one of them loads, Claude Code runs the rest of that turn on Haiku. That is right for these skills, because the turn that loads them does nothing but follow a template or fix a specific rule.

Every other skill leaves the model alone and runs on yours. Some look like lookups but load in the middle of other work, such as `typescript` and `tailwind`. A Haiku setting there would move the rest of your implementation turn to Haiku. Others need judgment, such as `react-code`.

A pinned skill runs the rest of the turn on its model even when you picked a stronger one, so a pin on judgment work would hold that work back. Set a model on a skill you write only when every use of it is template or rule-id work.

## What you control

- **Your session's model**, with `/model`. It runs every step that is not listed in the table, and everything that needs you.
- **The planner's model**, when `/gaia-plan` asks.
- **Effort**, with `/effort`, for your session and every sub-agent.

What each step costs shows up in the [cost and usage ledger](/models-and-context/cost/).
